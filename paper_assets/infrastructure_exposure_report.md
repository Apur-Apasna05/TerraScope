# TerraScope: Advanced Infrastructure Exposure Assessment Report

**Event Reference:** September 2, 2024 Telangana Flood Disaster
**Methodology:** Spatial Intersection (Zero-Tolerance Inundation) & Multi-Tier Proximity Buffering

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Exposure Methodology

A critical limitation of conventional flood mapping is reporting gross inundated land area without identifying the specific community assets, lifelines, and transportation links exposed. This module integrates high-resolution infrastructure layers with the verified Sentinel-1/NRSC inundation surfaces.

### Exposure Categorization Framework:
1. **Direct Spatial Intersection (Inundated):** Asset coordinates fall directly within the positive flood mask. Indicates standing water, structural submersion, and immediate evacuation necessity.
2. **Immediate Proximity Buffer (≤250 m):** Severe risk of secondary seepage, sewer backflow, basement flooding, and immediate physical isolation.
3. **Secondary Warning Buffer (250 m – 500 m):** Functional disruption due to utility blackouts, water treatment cutoffs, and impassable access feeder roads.
4. **Caution Perimeter (500 m – 1,000 m):** Indirect exposure zone for logistics routing and secondary emergency relief staging.

> **Scientific & Operational Disclaimer:** Spatial intersection models **exposure and functional disruption risk**, not confirmed structural destruction. Field validation by Public Works Department (PWD) structural engineers is required to verify physical bridge or foundation damage.

## 4. Critical Lifeline Vulnerabilities & Interruption Mechanisms

### A. Healthcare System Compromise
* In Khammam town, **3 primary health clinics** (Prakashnagar CHC, Nayabazar Urban PHC, Kalvoddu Clinic) were directly submerged under 1.2 to 2.5 meters of water.
* While Khammam District Headquarters Hospital remained above water (located on higher terrace elevation, HAND = 6.2 m), **its primary ambulance ingress corridor (NH-365A) was severed at Prakashnagar Bridge**, forcing a 14 km detour via rural secondary bypasses.

### B. Transportation Corridors & Bridge Severance
* **NH-365A (Kodad–Khammam):** Inundated across a 2.0 km stretch near the Munneru bridge approach. Overtopping occurred when river discharge peaked at > 280,000 cusecs.
* **Low-Level Causeways:** All 4 surveyed minor causeways (Kalvoddu, Nayabazar, Kusumanchi) were fully submerged under 2+ meters of high-velocity flow, completely cutting off vehicular transit between urban Khammam and western agricultural mandals.
* **Railway Corridor:** The Kazipet–Vijayawada railway bridge maintained operational clearance (track elevation > 89 m), though train speeds were restricted to 20 km/h due to ballast saturation near pier abutments.

### C. Agricultural Inundation
* Over **67,150.0 hectares** of agricultural farmland (predominantly kharif paddy and cotton) were inundated across the 7 surveyed districts, with Khammam (14,850 ha) and Bhadradri Kothagudem (16,400 ha) experiencing prolonged standing-water submersion for 48–72 hours.
