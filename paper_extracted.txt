Abstract— This paper presents TerraScope, a multi -source 
geospatial intelligence framework for flood and fire monitoring 
and geographic risk assessment in Telangana, India. The 
framework fuses satellite imagery, rainfall and meteorological 
data, terrain, and population and infrastructure layers within a 
common spatial workflow. Flood extent is derived from 
Sentinel-1 SAR and optical water indices, fire hotspots from 
MODIS/VIIRS active -fire data, and a hazard –exposure–
vulnerability index ranks districts by relative risk. A web 
interface provides district-level 2D/3D exploration and alerts. 
Keywords— geospatial intel ligence, flood mapping, fire 
monitoring, Sentinel-1, remote sensing, GIS, data fusion, risk 
assessment. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Environmental hazards, particularly floods and fires, pose 
significant challenges to regional resilience, ecological 
stability, and infrastructure safety. Their effective monitoring 
requires the integration of heterogeneous information sources, 
as individual satellite observations or meteorological 
measurements often provide only a partial understanding of 
evolving environmental condit ions. Although advances in 
remote sensing and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) 
have improved the acquisition and visualization of 
environmental data, the fragmented nature of these sources 
can constrain comprehensive spatial analysis and risk 
interpretation. In particular, identifying an environmental 
event alone is insufficient for understanding its potential 
implications without considering surrounding terrain, 
vegetation, population distribution, transportation networks, 
and critical infrastructure. This highlights the need for an 
integrated geospatial approach capable of correlating 
environmental observations with relevant geographical and 
atmospheric parameters to support contextualized disaster 
assessment. 
To address this challenge, this paper presents TerraScope, 
a Telangana -focused geospatial intelligence system for 
integrated environmental monitoring and disaster risk 
assessment.The system overlays hazard observations on 
terrain, population and infrastructure layers within a single 
interactive framework. Through state-level and district-level 
spatial exploration, TerraScope enables the examination of 
environmental events in relation to rainfall, temperature, wind 
conditions, terrain, vegetation, population, and infrastructure. 
Furthermore, AI -assisted analytical components facilitate 
event detection, multi -source information correlation, and 
geographical risk assessment, while an integrated alerting 
mechanism communicates identified events and associated 
risk information. By consolidating these capabilities within a 
regionally focused platform, this work demonstrates an 
integrated approach to geospatial environmental intelligence, 
with the objective of supporting more informed spatial 
analysis and disaster-risk interpretation across Telangana. 
The main contributions of this paper are: (1) a unified flood–
fire framework that fuses satellite, meteorological, terrain and 
exposure data for Telangana; (2) a Random Forest flood 
classifier validated with spatial block cross-validation against 
⟨reference lay er⟩; (3) a transparent hazard –exposure–
vulnerability index for district-level relative risk; and (4) an 
interactive 2D/3D interface with alerts. 
II. RELATED WORK 
Satellite remote sensing plays an important role in flood 
monitoring, particularly in regions with limited ground-based 
observations. Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) imagery 
enables the identification of surface -water changes under 
different weather and illumination conditions [1]. The 
National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC), ISRO, has 
conducted satellite-based flood mapping in Telangana [2] . 
Recent research has also explored Sentinel -1 SAR imagery 
and machine -learning techniques for flood inundation 
mapping along the Godavari River, demonstrating the 
applicability of data -driven methods for ident ifying flood-
affected regions [3]. 
Satellite-based fire monitoring supports the identification and 
geographical analysis of active fire locations. Instruments 
such as the Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer 

=== PAGE 2 ===
(MODIS) and Visible Infrared Imaging Ra diometer Suite 
(VIIRS) provide thermal observations for detecting fire 
hotspots [4], [5]. In India, the Forest Survey of India (FSI) 
uses these satellite observations for near-real-time forest fire 
monitoring and alert dissemination [6]. Integrating fire-event 
information with meteorological conditions, vegetation, and 
nearby settlements provides additional context for assessing 
potential fire exposure. 
Geographic Information Systems (GIS) enable the 
integration of satellite observations, weather parameters, and 
geographical datasets for environmental analysis. Combining 
layers such as terrain, vegetation, population, roads, and 
infrastructure helps examine the spatial relationship between 
environmental hazards and surrounding features. Building on 
this approach, TerraScope integrates flood observations, fire-
event information, meteorological data, and geographical 
layers into a unified Telangana -focused platform, 
incorporating AI-assisted analysis, spatial visualization, and 
alert management for regional environmental monitoring and 
risk assessment. Existing platforms such as Copernicus EMS, 
the Global Flood Monitoring service, Global Forest Watch 
and ISRO’s Bhuvan provide hazard maps at national or 
global scale, but they generally treat floods and fires 
separately and do not combine them with district -level 
exposure layers for Telangana. TerraScope addresses this 
gap. 
III. PROPOSED SYSTEM 
TerraScope is a Telangana-centric geospatial intelligence 
platform designed to integrate, visualize, and analyse 
environmental and geographical datasets for disaster 
monitoring and risk assessment. The system follows a 
modular architecture consisting of data acquisition, geospatial 
processing, multi -hazard analysis, AI -assisted intelligence, 
and visualization with alert management. These components 
operate within a common spatial framework to support the 
monitoring of two major environmental hazards: floods and 
fires, with geographical exploration at state and district levels. 
The data acquisition component collects satellite imagery, 
rainfall measurements, temperature, wind speed and direction, 
fire-event records, and flood -related observations from 
relevant data sources. It also incorporates geographical 
datasets such as administrative boundaries, elevation, 
vegetation, population distribution, roads, and infrastructure. 
These datasets are processed and integrated to establish spatial 
consistency and enable their association with relevant 
geographical locations across Telangana. 
The environmental analysis component supports separate 
monitoring workflows for floods and fires. Flood monitoring 
uses satellite-derived observations, rainfall information, and 
geographical characteristics to identify and examine 
potentially inundated regions. Fire monitoring combines fire-
event information with meteorological conditions, vegetation 
distribution, and nearby settlements to examine potential fire-
related risks. AI-assisted analytical capabilities support event 
identification, multi-source data correlation, and geographical 
risk assessment by considering hazard observations alongside 
relevant environmental and geographical parameters. 
The visualization and alert management component 
provides an interactive interface through two -dimensional 
maps and three -dimensional geographical views. Users can  
explore Telangana and its districts while examining satellite 
imagery, weather conditions, hazard locations, and 
geographical exposure layers. The alert mechanism 
communicates identified environmental events and associated 
risk information.  
IV. METHODOLOGY 
The methodology adopted in TerraScope follows an 
integrated geospatial processing framework for environmental 
monitoring and disaster risk assessment across Telangana. 
The framework combines heterogeneous environmental 
observations, meteorological parameter s, and geographical 
datasets to facilitate hazard identification, spatial correlation, 
and geographical exposure assessment. The overall 
processing workflow consists of data acquisition, geospatial 
preprocessing, flood and fire analysis, multi -source data 
integration, risk assessment, and visualization. Each stage 
contributes to transforming environmental observations into 
geographically contextualized information for regional 
disaster intelligence. 
A. Study Area and Data Acquisition 
The study area comprises the geographical extent of 
Telangana, India, including its administrative districts. 
Environmental and geographical datasets are acquired from 
relevant satellite observation platforms, meteorological 
services, and geospatial repositories. The pri mary inputs 
include satellite imagery, rainfall measurements, temperature, 
wind speed, wind direction, fire -event observations, and 
flood-related information. Additional spatial datasets 
representing administrative boundaries, elevation, vegetation, 
population distribution, transportation networks, and 
infrastructure are incorporated to establish the geographical 
context of detected environmental events. 
Table I lists the datasets used. Sentinel -1 C -band SAR 
(VV/VH, Ground Range Detected, Interferometric Wi de 
mode) provides all -weather water detection, and Sentinel -2 
optical imagery provides NDWI and vegetation indices. 
Rainfall is taken from [CHIRPS / IMD gridded rainfall] and 
wind and temperature from [ERA5 -Land / IMD]. Terrain 
parameters (elevation, slope ) are derived from the SRTM 
digital elevation model. Active fire detections come from 
NASA FIRMS (MODIS and VIIRS). Population is taken from 
WorldPop, and road networks from OpenStreetMap. All 
layers are clipped to the Telangana district boundaries from 
[source] and resampled to a common [30 m / 100 m] grid.

B. Geospatial Preprocessing and Data Integration 
The acquired datasets may exhibit differences in 
coordinate reference systems, spatial resolutions, temporal 
attributes, and data formats. Geospatial preprocessing is 
therefore performed to facilitate consistent spatial 
representation and interoperability. The processing stage 
involves coordinate reference system transformation, spatial 
alignment, attribute organization, and geograph ical 
association with the administrative boundaries of Telangana.

a common interval to facilitate comparative analysis. Min –

The flood -monitoring component examines satellite -
derived surface observations in conjunction with rainfall 
information and terrain characteristics to identify and interpret 
potentially inundated regions. Satellite imagery provides the 
spatial basis for examining water -related surface changes, 
while rainfall observations contribute meteorological context 
to flood-related conditions. Elevation and terrain information 
further support the geographical interpretation of potentially 
affected areas.

D. Fire Detection and Meteorological Correlation 
The fire -monitoring component utilizes available fire -
event observations to identify the geographical locations of 
detected fire activity. These locations are examined in 
conjunction with meteorological parameters, particularly 
wind speed and wind direction, to understand the surrounding 
atmospheric conditions. Vegetatio n distribution, terrain 
characteristics, and nearby settlements and infrastructure are 
incorporated to provide additional geographical context. 
The integration of fire -event locations with wind-related 
information facilitates the examination of potential downwind 
exposure. Vegetation and land -cover characteristics provide 
contextual information regarding the surrounding 
environment, while population and infrastructure layers assist 
in identifying geographically exposed features. This 
combined analysis supports fire-related risk assessment and 
the spatial interpretation of environmental conditions. Wind 
direction is treated as a contextual parameter for exposure 
analysis rather than a deterministic prediction of fire 
propagation.

or non-flood. The input feature vector X contains Sentinel-1 
VV and VH backscatter (dB), NDWI, [7 -day] cumulative 
rainfall, elevation, slope, and [HAND]. Training labels are 
derived from [the NRSC flood inundation  layer [2] for the 
(event/date)]. The labeled samples are split [70%/30%] into 
training and test sets using spatially separated blocks to avoid 
inflated accuracy from spatial autocorrelation. The model uses 
[200] trees with [default / tuned] hyperparameter s. 
Performance is assessed with precision, recall, F1-score and

G. Geospatial Visualization and Alert Generation 
The final stage presents the processed environmental 
information through an interactive geospatial visualization 
interface. Satellite observations, meteorological parameters, 
fire-event locations, flood -related information, and 
geographical exposure layers are represented within a unified 
geographical environment. Two -dimensional map 
representations and three-dimensional visualization facilitate 
spatial exploration across Telangana, while administrative 
boundary integration enables district-level navigation. 
The alert management component communicates 
identified environmental events and associated risk 
information through the platform interface. This facilitates 
access to geographically contextualized information and 
enables users to examine  relevant environmental conditions 
and potentially exposed locations. 
Together, these stages convert raw observations into 
geographically contextualized hazard and exposure 
information for each district of Telangana. 
V. RESULT AND DISCUSSION 
 
The TerraScope prototype lets users move from a state-wide 
view to individual districts and inspect hazard layers in 2D 
and 3D alongside weather and exposure data. Viewing these 
layers together provides a consolidated picture of 
environmental conditions at different geographical scales. 
 
The system supports fire monitoring through the visualization 
of fire events and their corresponding geographical locations. 
Meteorological parameters, including temperature and wind 
conditions, together with vegetation distribution and ne arby 
settlements, provide additional context for examining 
potential fire -related risks. Similarly, the flood -monitoring 
functionality supports the identification and visualization of 
flood-affected areas using available satellite imagery and 
flood-related information. Rainfall observations and 
geographical layers, including population distribution, road 
networks, and infrastructure, contribute to understanding the 
potential exposure of surrounding regions to flood 
conditions. 
The integration of population, vegetation, transportation, and 
infrastructure layers enhances the geographical context of 
hazard monitoring. These datasets help identify areas where 
environmental events may have implications for human 
settlements and essential infrastructure. AI -assisted 
analytical capabilities further support environmental event 
identification, correlation of relevant information, and 
geographical risk assessment. The alert and notification 
functionality provides a mechanism for communicating 
identified events and potential risk conditions to users. 
The implementation shows that fire and flood monitoring can 
be combined with exposure information in one regional 
system. However, the operational effectiveness of the system 
must be established through systematic evaluation  using 
independently validated reference datasets. Quantitative 
measures such as detection accuracy, false -positive rate, 
spatial agreement, and alert response time would provide a

stronger basis for assessing system performance. These 
measurements are not  claimed here in the absence of 
experimental validation results. 
VI. CONCLUSION 
TerraScope is a Telangana -focused system that supports 
environmental monitoring and disaster -risk assessment by 
placing hazard observations in their terrain, population and 
infrastructure context. Users can monitor fire and flood 
events and explore conditions through state-level and district-
level navigation with 3D visualization. 
By integrating environmental parameters such as 
temperature, rainfall, wind conditions, and vegetation with 
population distribution, roads, and infrastructure, TerraScope 
provides valuable geographical context for understanding 
potential disaster impacts. Its AI -assisted analytical 
capabilities support event identification, information 
correlation, and geographical risk assessment, while the alert 
and notification features help communicate relevant 
environmental events and potential risk conditions. 
The project demonstrates the practical application of 
geospatial technologies and artificial intelligence in 
addressing regional environmental monitoring challenges. 
Rather than switching between separate data sources, users 
can interpret them together in one interface.  TerraScope 
therefore provides a foundation for more accessible, 
integrated, and data -driven environmental monitoring and 
disaster-management support in Telangana.
